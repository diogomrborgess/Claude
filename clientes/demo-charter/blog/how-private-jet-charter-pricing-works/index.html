How private jet charter pricing works

14 October 20265 min read

A charter quote is a single figure, but it is built from a handful of parts. Knowing them helps you compare two quotes fairly and spot the one that will grow later.

Flight time

The largest part. Every aircraft has an hourly rate that covers the aircraft, crew, fuel and maintenance. Larger and faster aircraft cost more per hour, so the right size for the trip matters more than anything else.

Positioning

If the aircraft is not based where you depart, it has to fly there first and home afterwards. Those empty hours are part of the price. An aircraft based near you is often cheaper than a better deal parked two hours away.

Airport and handling fees

Landing, parking, passenger fees and the ground handler at each airport. They vary widely: a small regional airfield costs a fraction of a major hub at peak time.

Crew and waiting

On a same-day return the aircraft waits for you. Over several days, you either pay for the crew to stay or for the aircraft to go home and come back. We price both and show you the cheaper one.

Tax and extras

VAT depends on the route. Extras are anything beyond the standard: special catering, de-icing in winter, satellite internet. A clear quote lists what is included and names what could be added.

Empty legs

When an aircraft must fly empty to its next client, that leg is sold at a reduced price. It suits flexible travellers: the date and route are fixed by someone else’s trip, and it can change if theirs does.

How to read a quote

  • Check the aircraft type, year and operator, not only the price.
  • Ask whether the figure is fixed or an estimate.
  • Read the cancellation terms before you confirm.
